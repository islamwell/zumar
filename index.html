Sentence Structure

This verse is a nominal sentence — a subject paired with a predicate, no verb needed

تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْحَكِيمِ
Subject (Mubtada)
تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ
The revelation of the Book
⟵⟶
Predicate (Khabar)
مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْحَكِيمِ
is from Allah, the Almighty, the All-Wise

Notice: There is no verb in this verse. In Arabic, a nominal sentence (a sentence that starts with a noun) makes a timeless, factual statement — this revelation is from Allah, always was, and always will be.

4

Key Themes

The deep meanings embedded in just six words

📖
Divine Origin
The Quran is not the work of a human being. It is a revelation sent down directly from the Creator of everything.
⏳
Gradual Revelation
The word "Tanzeel" (Form II) tells us the Quran was sent down stage by stage over 23 years — not all at once — to guide people step by step.
⚡
Power and Wisdom Together
Allah pairs "The Almighty" with "The All-Wise" — His power is never random. Every decree is backed by perfect wisdom.
🔗
Authority of the Message
By opening the Surah this way, Allah establishes that everything that follows carries the highest possible authority — it is from Him.
